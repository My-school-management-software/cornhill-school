Import existing marks from Excel

Choose your workbook and term. CORNHILL matches the learners and subjects in your assigned class. Learner records are never changed.

Use your original CORNHILL Excel arrangement — do not rearrange the workbook
  1. Keep one worksheet for the assigned class. The importer recognises class names such as Baby, Middle, Top, P.1 through P.7, and Primary One through Primary Seven.
  2. Keep the identity columns first: SN, ID, PHOTO, NAME (or PUPIL'S NAMES), SEX, LINS. The alternative headings Pupil Code, LIN, Learner Name are also accepted. ID is matched as a pupil code where possible; otherwise the importer verifies the learner using LIN or a unique name.
  3. Keep the subject headers and their order exactly as they appear in your class workbook. Nursery and P1–P5 use three periods; P6 uses Set I–III and P7 uses Set I–IV. The importer recognises the original short subject codes, including headers without a period number when the preceding total column identifies the period.
  4. Keep one learner per row. Summary columns such as TT, AGG, DIV, PN and POTN are calculated/review columns; they are not imported as subject marks. The ready-made template follows this same class-sheet arrangement.
  5. Enter marks as numbers. Leave a cell empty if the learner has not yet been assessed. Empty cells are skipped; they are not saved as zero.
  6. Preview the workbook first. Correct every unmatched learner or subject shown in the review before importing. Existing marks are protected unless you deliberately tick Replace marks that already exist.

Important: A real score of 0 is entered as 0. Do not type a dash for a missing mark. For P4–P7 only, X means absent where the school uses the absent-mark option.

9. Privacy, security and acceptable-use policy

School data: Learner, guardian, staff, academic and finance information is confidential school information. Access is limited to authorised users according to assigned roles.

Passwords: Every staff member must use a private password. Do not share passwords or send them in ordinary messages. Use the secure reset process when access is lost.

Accuracy: Staff must enter accurate records and report errors to the administrator. Financial transactions and official learner records must not be changed to conceal mistakes.

Devices: Sign out on shared computers, protect phones with a screen lock and do not leave school records open to unauthorised people.

Offline use: Online records remain the source of truth. An administrator or teacher who previously loaded the school records online may have a recent, read-only class-list snapshot available for up to 24 hours without internet. Administrator snapshots can include the school-wide learner register; teacher snapshots are limited to the assigned class. This local browser copy may contain learner names, pupil codes, LIN and class information. Use it only on a school-managed, screen-locked device and sign out after use. Offline mode does not support admissions, edits, marks entry/import, report publication, finance, parent messaging, staff or attendance changes. No changes are queued or automatically synchronised. Reconnect before updating official school records. Parent access remains online-only.

Notifications: Parent SMS requires guardian consent and an approved school-configured provider. No SMS or push service is promised by this release.

Retention and incidents: The school administrator should maintain approved backup, retention and data-breach response procedures and comply with applicable Ugandan data-protection requirements.

Receipt printers — five models to consider

Fingerprint terminal connection instructions — CORNHILL setup

Recommended first purchase: a genuine ZKTeco K40/K40 Pro attendance terminal with TCP/IP Ethernet and USB-host export, subject to the seller's live demonstration and exact firmware confirmation. The other four terminal options are alternatives, not devices that must all be purchased.

  1. Before purchase: ask the seller to demonstrate a fingerprint enrolment, clock-in and clock-out on the exact unit. Obtain the model/variant, serial number, firmware version, user capacity, log capacity, warranty, Windows software/SDK details and a sample CSV export in writing.
  2. Install at school: connect the terminal to a stable power supply and the school office LAN using Ethernet where available. Set the device date/time and timezone to Africa/Kampala (EAT, UTC+3). Protect the administrator menu with a private device password.
  3. Register staff: in Staff Attendance, create one device-user mapping per staff member. Use a unique numeric machine user ID and map it to the correct Cornhill staff profile. Do not match records by name alone.
  4. Enrol fingerprints: enrol staff on the terminal itself. Keep biometric templates on the terminal; never upload fingerprint images/templates to Cornhill or share the device administrator password.
  5. First data transfer: use the terminal's USB-host function to export attendance logs to a USB flash drive, then upload the CSV in Staff Attendance. USB-host normally means a flash drive plugged into the terminal; it does not automatically mean a direct USB cable connection to the office PC.
  6. Configure schedules: create the school's work schedules, grace periods, breaks and working days, then assign each staff member. Review the daily attendance summary and correction requests.
  7. Verify every import: preview the file, confirm device serial and staff-ID matches, import once, then compare the Cornhill totals with the terminal's own report. Re-importing the same export should not create duplicate events.
  8. Network sync later: if the exact device/firmware supports a documented ADMS/PUSH/API integration, enable it only after a supplier demonstration and a secured endpoint adapter has been implemented and tested. Do not expose the terminal directly to the public internet or place database/service keys in the device.

Operational fallback: if the terminal, power or network fails, keep a signed manual attendance register and have an administrator enter/approve the correction. The five-minute background attendance check evaluates stored records; it does not itself read the fingerprint machine.

Privacy: before enrolling staff, issue the school's biometric-data notice, restrict device administration to authorised personnel, define retention and dispute procedures, and provide an approved non-biometric fallback.

Connection status: device-independent attendance workflows and CSV import are prepared. Physical terminal connection, direct network push and vendor SDK compatibility remain pending until a device is purchased and tested.

Small USB fingerprint readers — five compact models

These are desktop fingerprint capture devices similar in size to readers used at registration counters. They are not the same as a standalone attendance clock. They require a Windows/Android SDK and a local integration program; they will not work with the browser alone.

  1. ZKTeco ZK9500 — compact USB optical reader; ZKTeco provides a Windows SDK for it. Device details.
  2. ZKTeco ZK6500 — USB fingerprint reader supported by ZKTeco's ZKFinger SDK. SDK compatibility list.
  3. ZKTeco ZK8500R — USB fingerprint and ID/Mifare card reader for registration workflows. Device details.
