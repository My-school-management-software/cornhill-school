7. Privacy, security and acceptable-use policy

School data: Learner, guardian, staff, academic and finance information is confidential school information. Access is limited to authorised users according to assigned roles.

Passwords: Every staff member must use a private password. Do not share passwords or send them in ordinary messages. Use the secure reset process when access is lost.

Accuracy: Staff must enter accurate records and report errors to the administrator. Financial transactions and official learner records must not be changed to conceal mistakes.

Devices: Sign out on shared computers, protect phones with a screen lock and do not leave school records open to unauthorised people.

Offline use: Offline mode currently provides the cached application shell only. It does not authorise offline payments or guarantee that learner records and marks are synchronised.

Notifications: Parent SMS requires guardian consent and an approved school-configured provider. No SMS or push service is promised by this release.

Retention and incidents: The school administrator should maintain approved backup, retention and data-breach response procedures and comply with applicable Ugandan data-protection requirements.

This is an in-app policy summary for school review, not legal advice or a substitute for a formally approved school privacy notice.

8. Hardware buying guide — recommended models

Important: These are researched purchase candidates, not devices already physically tested with CORNHILL. Ask the seller for the exact model, connection type, original drivers, warranty and a live demonstration before paying. Hardware prices and availability change. Do not buy a fingerprint device solely because it says “USB fingerprint”.

Receipt printers — five models to consider

  1. Xprinter XP-P300 — budget portable choice (58mm). USB + Bluetooth, rechargeable battery, designed for mobile receipts. A good first model to investigate for Android and Windows use. Manufacturer specifications.
  2. Epson TM-P20II — premium portable choice (58mm). Bluetooth or Wi-Fi versions; supports mobile printing and is designed for portable POS use. More expensive category; consider if durability and manufacturer support are priorities. Manufacturer specifications.
  3. Xprinter XP-80U — value office-counter choice (80mm). USB or USB + Ethernet variants, 200mm/s printing; suitable for a fixed bursar's desk. Manufacturer specifications.
  4. Rongta RP80 series — alternative 80mm counter printer. Several RP80 variants exist; confirm the exact USB/LAN/Bluetooth interface before ordering. Manufacturer manuals and drivers.
  5. Epson TM-T20III — established office-counter choice (80mm). USB/serial or Ethernet variants, high-speed printing and automatic cutter; choose Ethernet if you want the office printer shared over the school network. Epson Africa specifications.

My purchasing recommendation: Start by comparing the Xprinter XP-P300 for portable receipts and the Xprinter XP-80U for the main bursar's desk. If the budget allows and you prefer an established office printer brand, choose the Epson TM-T20III. The school can buy one printer first; the software already has 58mm and 80mm print layouts.

Uganda price guide (checked 4 November 2026): a Kampala marketplace listing specifically identifies the Xprinter XP-P300 at about UGX 350,000. Other local listings for generic 58mm Bluetooth printers vary (one supplier lists a non-model-specific 58mm unit at UGX 150,000–215,000); do not assume those are XP-P300 units. XP-P300 local listing · Uganda thermal printer catalogue. I could not verify a current local price for the exact XP-80U or Epson models, so request written quotations before budgeting.

Fingerprint attendance terminals — five models to compare

  1. ZKTeco K40 — my budget recommendation for CORNHILL. Fingerprint attendance terminal with TCP/IP, USB-host and backup battery. Published capacity varies by K40 firmware/variant and SSR configuration: official regional specifications list up to 3,000 fingerprints and 100,000 transactions, while other K40 listings show 1,000 fingerprints and 80,000 records. A Kampala supplier advertises UGX 380,000; another Uganda listing advertises about UGX 745,000, so treat UGX 380,000–745,000 as indicative advertised prices, not a guaranteed quote. Ask the seller to identify the exact K40/K40 Pro model and show its device information screen before purchase. Official regional specifications · Uganda supplier listing · Alternative Uganda listing.
  2. ZKTeco K50 — alternative compact attendance terminal. TCP/IP and USB-host, backup battery, up to 3,000 fingerprints and 100,000 transactions in the published specification. Manufacturer specifications.
  3. ZKTeco MB20 — fingerprint plus face/card options. TCP/IP and USB-host; optional Wi-Fi; 500 fingerprint capacity and 50,000 records in the listed specification. Manufacturer specifications.
  4. ZKTeco iClock680 — higher-capacity option with a Uganda listing. Fingerprint terminal with USB-host and TCP/IP; a Uganda listing describes 8,000 fingerprints and 200,000 records. Confirm the exact unit and optional connectivity on the supplier quote. Uganda listing and specifications.
  5. ZKTeco SpeedFace-V5L — premium option. Face and fingerprint verification, TCP/IP, up to 10,000 fingerprints and 200,000 transactions on the current listed version. Likely more than the school needs for approximately 30 staff. Manufacturer specifications.

My purchasing recommendation: Choose a genuine ZKTeco K40/K40 Pro with TCP/IP and USB-host for one main-office attendance terminal for around 30 staff. Before paying, require a live clock-in/clock-out demonstration, a sample attendance export (CSV/Excel), exact firmware/version, warranty and confirmation that the model works with the chosen Windows 10/11 attendance software or documented SDK. Our school integration still needs testing against the actual unit; USB-host usually means a USB flash drive connection, not necessarily direct USB cable communication to a PC. Network TCP/IP is the preferred integration route to investigate.

Fingerprint terminal connection instructions — CORNHILL setup

Recommended first purchase: a genuine ZKTeco K40/K40 Pro attendance terminal with TCP/IP Ethernet and USB-host export, subject to the seller's live demonstration and exact firmware confirmation. The other four terminal options are alternatives, not devices that must all be purchased.

  1. Before purchase: ask the seller to demonstrate a fingerprint enrolment, clock-in and clock-out on the exact unit. Obtain the model/variant, serial number, firmware version, user capacity, log capacity, warranty, Windows software/SDK details and a sample CSV export in writing.
  2. Install at school: connect the terminal to a stable power supply and the school office LAN using Ethernet where available. Set the device date/time and timezone to Africa/Kampala (EAT, UTC+3). Protect the administrator menu with a private device password.
  3. Register staff: in Staff Attendance, create one device-user mapping per staff member. Use a unique numeric machine user ID and map it to the correct Cornhill staff profile. Do not match records by name alone.
  4. Enrol fingerprints: enrol staff on the terminal itself. Keep biometric templates on the terminal; never upload fingerprint images/templates to Cornhill or share the device administrator password.
  5. First data transfer: use the terminal's USB-host function to export attendance logs to a USB flash drive, then upload the CSV in Staff Attendance. USB-host normally means a flash drive plugged into the terminal; it does not automatically mean a direct USB cable connection to the office PC.
  6. Configure schedules: create the school's work schedules, grace periods, breaks and working days, then assign each staff member. Review the daily attendance summary and correction requests.
  7. Verify every import: preview the file, confirm device serial and staff-ID matches, import once, then compare the Cornhill totals with the terminal's own report. Re-importing the same export should not create duplicate events.
  8. Network sync later: if the exact device/firmware supports a documented ADMS/PUSH/API integration, enable it only after a supplier demonstration and a secured endpoint adapter has been implemented and tested. Do not expose the terminal directly to the public internet or place database/service keys in the device.

Operational fallback: if the terminal, power or network fails, keep a signed manual attendance register and have an administrator enter/approve the correction. The five-minute background attendance check evaluates stored records; it does not itself read the fingerprint machine.

Privacy: before enrolling staff, issue the school's biometric-data notice, restrict device administration to authorised personnel, define retention and dispute procedures, and provide an approved non-biometric fallback.

Connection status: device-independent attendance workflows and CSV import are prepared. Physical terminal connection, direct network push and vendor SDK compatibility remain pending until a device is purchased and tested.

Small USB fingerprint readers — five compact models

These are desktop fingerprint capture devices similar in size to readers used at registration counters. They are not the same as a standalone attendance clock. They require a Windows/Android SDK and a local integration program; they will not work with the browser alone.

  1. ZKTeco ZK9500 — compact USB optical reader; ZKTeco provides a Windows SDK for it. Device details.
  2. ZKTeco ZK6500 — USB fingerprint reader supported by ZKTeco's ZKFinger SDK. SDK compatibility list.
  3. ZKTeco ZK8500R — USB fingerprint and ID/Mifare card reader for registration workflows. Device details.
  4. SecuGen Hamster Pro 20 — compact USB reader; SecuGen offers SDKs for Windows, Linux and Android. Device and SDK details.
  5. SecuGen Hamster Pro 10 — another USB reader in the manufacturer's Windows driver/SDK family. Supported driver models.

Attendance hardware is not automatically biometric-ID hardware: the small USB readers above are options for enrolling/verifying fingerprints at a computer, while the K40/K50/MB20/F18/SpeedFace are attendance terminals that store staff templates and clock events. For CORNHILL's first attendance setup, I recommend the K40 terminal, not a USB reader.

Privacy and security: fingerprints are sensitive biometric data. The system should store a protected biometric reference/template or a device-generated staff ID—not raw fingerprint photographs in the school database. Staff must be informed and an appropriate lawful basis and access controls established before collection.
